The problem

Banks have dozens of AI apps in flight and no shared way to decide which ones should ship.

Copilot add-ons, vendor tools, and in-house models each arrive with a demo and a promise. What is missing is one place that knows who owns each app, what it is supposed to save, which approvals it has, and whether the model inside is good enough to trust with customers. This project builds that place and runs one use case through it end to end.

A governed registry

40 apps across Copilot, Abacus, custom builds, and managed apps move through nine stages. Every move is checked on the server against the lifecycle rules and the person's role. Nobody can approve their own submission. SYNTHETIC

Evidence before release

Support Triage routes banking messages to auto-handling, human review, or abstain. Three configs were scored on the same frozen cases, a deterministic policy decides each route, and release gates decide whether a model may go live.

Control after release

A budget ledger reserves money before every model call, daily signals watch for drift, and an incident pauses automation and rolls back to human review. All of it is visible in one console.

The decision

Withhold the LLM. Keep the classifier in production.

  1. gemini-3.8-flash passes 5 of 10 release gates as a full candidate and 4 of 6 as the proposed cohort. MEASURED
  2. Its macro F1 is 0.885 against a floor of 0.893, and 4 sensitive intents miss the 0.95 recall gate, the lowest at 0.60. MEASURED
  3. Where the LLM and the classifier agree, auto-routed precision rises to 0.976, and the LLM routes 90% of out-of-domain risk cases to abstain against 0% for the classifier. MEASURED
  4. In a 14-day shadow replay the models agreed on 85.5% of labels SIMULATED for $1.65 of model spend MEASURED; the cohort would have added about $151 of net value ASSUMPTION.
  5. That gain does not justify releasing below the sensitive-recall bar. The next investment is targeted: fix the card-payment versus direct-debit confusions and re-evaluate, rather than buying a bigger model.

Incident response

Drift was caught the day it began, and automation stood down.

A drill on the production classifier: alert thresholds were frozen from a baseline window before 40 authored drift cases were injected on simulated days 10 to 12.

  1. Days 1–7BaselineThresholds set from the mean and spread of each daily signal, then frozen.
  2. Day 10Drift beginsParaphrased sensitive and out-of-domain messages join the traffic. FIXTURE
  3. Day 10Incident openedReview rate 0.2177 against a threshold of 0.2172: detected 0 days after the drift began. MEASURED
  4. Day 10Pause and roll backThe governance admin pauses automation; every case goes to human review.
  5. Day 13ResumeA shadow check passes every threshold, and automation resumes.

The honest caveat: the margin was 0.0006 and no other signal breached, so the report recommends a drift-specific signal before relying on these alerts. Source: docs/incident_report.md

Engineering decisions

Choices, the alternatives, and why.

DecisionAlternatives consideredWhy
A deterministic six-rule policy decides every routeTrust the LLM's self-reported confidenceSelf-reported confidence is neither calibrated nor auditable. Thresholds are tuned on development data and frozen before evaluation. (D6, D15)
The frozen set is used once per configIterate against the test setRe-running against the test set quietly tunes to it; a re-run needs a logged reason. (D10)
A ledger reserves budget before every model callBilling alerts after the factReserve, send, settle exactly once; lost responses are held, never retried. Total model spend was capped at $10. (D11, D13, D20)
Withhold the better-scoring LLMShip a narrowed cohort anywayThe cohort still failed macro F1 and one sensitive recall; the measured result wins over the demo story. (D30)
IAP directly on Cloud Run; the backend re-verifies every assertionA load balancer in front of IAPNo load balancer cost, and a forged header is rejected at the edge and again in the app. (D33, D35)
Analytics export loads staging tables, then MERGEsStreaming insertsRe-running the nightly export cannot double-count: the same row count after two runs. (D34, D37)
The public app runs the unchanged backend in the browserA read-only snapshot; a hosted backend and databaseVisitors can act, nobody can deface it, it costs nothing, and a parity test holds it to the server. (D36, D38)

Limitations

What this does not prove.

  • Single-rater labels. The 60 risk-suite cases and the 40 drift cases were written and labeled by one person. AUTHORED
  • Simulated traffic. Shadow mode replays the Banking77 test split over 14 simulated days, not live customers. SIMULATED
  • Benchmark versus production. Banking77 messages are short, single-intent, and English. Real queues are messier.
  • Assumed economics. Volumes, minutes, labor rates, and misroute costs are placeholders a finance partner would supply. ASSUMPTION
  • A sample organization. The 40-app registry is generated, not a real bank's portfolio. SYNTHETIC
  • No live model calls in the public app. Its numbers come from the committed real runs; the Google Cloud deployment was torn down after verification.
